ELECTRONIC LOAD - INTEGRATION TEST PLAN

Firmware: CV tuning v3 TRIAL
Kp = 0.002 A/V; Ki = 0.008 A/(V*s); Kd = 0 (PI).
CV acquisition target = 50 ms; rise/fall = 0.015/0.030 A/s.
Working comparison baseline: v2 (Ki 0.005, rise 0.010), test_0056.csv.
V3 is a trial, not a demonstrated stability improvement.

1. PROBES AND STARTUP
Disconnect ALL supplies and USB before soldering or changing connections.
Fit all four external probes. Firmware now uses RN3440 nominal R25=10k,
B25/50=4100 K, not 3950 K. Their datasheet specifies +/-10% R25, so this
is not individual probe calibration. Keep bare leads electrically insulated.
Source: https://media.jaycar.com.au/product/resources/RN3440_datasheetMain_112446.pdf

After cooling from soldering, start with bench supply OFF. Require startup pass.
Use 'temps' in serial monitor. All six heatsink and four external readings must
be valid and plausible. Gently warm ONE external probe at a time and repeat temps:
only the corresponding external channel should rise, then fall after release.
The sensor thermal time constant is nominally 15 s; response is not instant.
Open/short/invalid external readings now cause startup or sensor faults rather
than a plausible cold reading. All four external probes are required.
Compare to a reference thermometer before relying on absolute external temperature.

2. CV BASELINE COMPARISON (DO THIS BEFORE CHANGING TEST POWER)
Supply: 5 V and 0.10 A current limit. It should begin in CV.
Load: CV, 4.00 V target, 0.20 A command ceiling, 50 s duration.
Save the CSV and note its filename and 'v3'. Compare with test_0056.csv:
time to approach 4 V; minimum voltage during transition; final-window mean,
min/max and peak-to-peak voltage. Use the same 22-49.6 s window initially.
V2 recorded: mean 3.9997 V, min 3.828 V, max 4.122 V in that window.
Require sustained regulation rather than repeated returns to 5 V or collapse.
A practical initial recorded-voltage target is 4 V +/-0.2 V after settling.
Reject v3 if it materially worsens the baseline ripple or creates sustained cycling.
SD logging is about 2 Hz and cannot establish absence of faster oscillation.
If available, use an oscilloscope for dynamics; do not infer stability from averages.
Do not raise source current to conceal unstable regulation.

3. STAGED CC LOAD TESTS
Advance one stage at a time only after the previous stage behaves normally.
Keep voltage at 5 V. Each first run lasts 10 seconds.

Load CC request    Supply current limit    Nominal load power
0.25 A             0.35 A                  1.25 W
0.50 A             0.65 A                  2.50 W
1.00 A             1.20 A                  5.00 W
2.00 A             2.30 A                 10.00 W

Record supply voltage/current, OLED voltage/current, CSV filename, all temperatures,
and stopped current before/after each run. Supply should stay in CV for these tests.
Stop on unexplained current growth, repeated mode switching, faults, a rapidly
rising or implausible sensor, or heatsink temperature reaching 50 C for this stage.
Do not bypass protection or raise thresholds to complete a stage.

Use CSV I1..I6 to check all six legs contribute and none remains near zero while
others carry the load. At 1 A total each leg should be near 0.167 A in a balanced
load; persistent differences above roughly 20% of the mean warrant investigation.
An initial total-current check is within max(0.020 A, 5% of request), allowing for
meter uncertainty. This is a screening criterion, not a specified product accuracy.
Only after passing, repeat the 1 A stage for 60 s to observe temperature and fans.
Confirm stopped current returns near its previously measured baseline, about 12 mA.

4. EXTEND CV COVERAGE ONLY AFTER STEP 2 PASSES
First: same 5 V / 0.10 A source, test targets 3.5 V then 4.5 V (50 s each,
0.20 A load ceiling). Change settings between runs. Save separate logs.
Then: 5 V / 0.20 A source, 4 V load target, 0.30 A ceiling, 60 s.
This last test increases load power to about 0.8 W and deliberately changes the
source operating point. It is not a remedy for a failed lower-current test.

These tests establish only the voltages, currents and durations actually measured.
They do NOT qualify 500 W, 30 A, full-voltage MOSFET safe operating area, or fault
protection under all conditions. Higher-power qualification needs a separate plan.
